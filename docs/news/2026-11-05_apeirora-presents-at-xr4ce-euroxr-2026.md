# ApeiroRA Presents at the XR4CE Workshop in Valencia

On November 3, 2026, we presented our paper "[From Consistent Data to Trustworthy Shared Context: AI-Assisted XR for Data-Center Operations](/publications/ai-assisted-xr-for-data-center-operations)" at the [2nd International Workshop on XR for Challenging Environments (XR4CE)](https://xr4ce-eur26.tech-experience.at/) in Valencia, Spain. The workshop took place at the Universitat Politècnica de València in the framework of [EuroXR 2026](https://www.euroxr.org/conference-2026) and the [VWP Annual Forum 2026](https://www.virtualworldsassociation.eu/actions/vwp-annual-forum-2026).

Under the theme "From Co-existence to Collaboration," the workshop explored how Extended Reality (XR) can support training, assistance, and collaboration in demanding operational environments. It continued the XR4CE series following the [CHI 2026 workshop in Barcelona](/news/2026-07-31_acm-chi-2026), where we introduced our earlier work on combining XR, the Asset Administration Shell (AAS), and AI-assisted interaction for data-center operations.

The new paper, authored by Dawid Lizurek, Bosen Zhou, Özlem Mis, Wanja Pasternak, and Frank Feinbube, examines how operators can assess whether the information presented in an XR interface is reliable enough to act on. Digital records, monitoring systems, and physical equipment can disagree, leaving technicians to resolve discrepancies through manual checks and on-site inspection.

Six interviews with data-center professionals highlighted fragmented tools, inconsistent records, and the effort required to gather and validate information. Participants linked the practical value of XR to access to current, trustworthy data. Building on these findings, the paper proposes five dimensions for communicating information reliability: source transparency, temporal validity, verification status, conflict awareness, and confidence indication. Together, these dimensions help explain where information comes from, when it was updated, how it has been checked, and which uncertainties remain.

A hypothetical cable-verification scenario illustrates the approach. An inventory record identifies one network port, while a more recent photograph suggests another. The proposed XR interface exposes the conflicting evidence, and an AI assistant explains its limitations and requests verification. An on-site technician then checks the connection and updates the shared record with supporting evidence. This scenario explores how operators and AI could coordinate verification while keeping the basis for each decision visible.

The five dimensions form a preliminary conceptual framework grounded in an exploratory interview study. Further research and controlled user studies are needed to assess whether the proposed indicators improve reliability assessment, decision-making, and operator trust. Within the Immersive Data Center Management project, this work sets out requirements for connecting AAS-based information, XR interfaces, and AI assistance in operational workflows.

We are pleased to have been part of the emerging research community around XR for Challenging Environments from the outset. As its next step, the community plans to develop a joint position paper and deepen collaboration among its members. We look forward to contributing our perspective from data-center operations and helping shape this shared research effort.
